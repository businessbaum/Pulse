# Lobby concept: the Roundhouse

The lobby is built on the story in lore.md. It starts dark and almost empty. Every story step wakes a machine or opens a door, and each of those gives the player a new way to upgrade, build or craft. The lobby is the progress display of the whole game.

## 1. The place

The Roundhouse is an old engine shed. The Rig stands on a turntable in the middle, and four arms around the turntable work on it without pause. A wall runs all the way round the hall, with one gate and six bay doors in it. Behind each bay door lies a room, and in each room one machine does its work. A door that is not open yet is a dark shutter.

## 2. How it plays

- The player walks a small robot through the Roundhouse. The machine stays on the turntable.
- **The workshop.** The middle of the turntable is a deck that turns, with the machine on it. Four arms stand around it: two weld, with sparks and a flickering light, and two tighten. Each picks a spot on the machine it can reach, works on it, lifts off and picks the next. Every few seconds all four lift clear, the deck turns the machine to another side, and they start again. It is a loop and changes nothing about the machine; it shows that the place is alive.
- Walking up to a machine brings up a small prompt from the bottom of the screen: the key to press, in Pulse green, and the machine's name. Pressing it opens that machine's screen.
- A marked pad on the floor at the gate starts a run. It is a dark half circle with its straight side against the gate and a Pulse-green rim along its arc. Standing in it fills a bar; a green half circle grows out from the gate in step with it.
- When the bar is full, a short scene plays: the arms fold away, the deck swings the machine round to face the robot, it rolls up to the robot, the robot jumps onto it, and the machine tears out through the gate into the dark. The open world picks it up from there without a cut: outside the gate lies the same dark ground the machine arrives on, the camera and the light change to those of the open world while it drives, and the dark closes in around the machine. The open world starts from that same picture and opens it again, with the machine still racing, and it brakes as it reaches the starting point.
- Coming home through the teleporter (machine-concept.md, section 9.4) is a scene too: the dark opens on the empty deck, light pours down onto it, the machine snaps into being, and the robot climbs out of it and jumps down toward the camera. Then the arms go back to work.
- **What stands in the way is as large as it looks.** The hall ends at the foot of its fourteen wall pieces, not at a circle inside them. A bench, a shelf, the furnace and a crate are boxes of their own size, turned as they stand; a lamp, a barrel and a machine are circles. The character slides along all of them.
- **The wall is closed all the way round.** Fourteen pieces make the ring. Where two meet stands a pillar, thicker and higher than both, so the ends of the pieces disappear inside it and no two faces lie in the same plane. On the side the camera looks in from, the same wall and the same pillars are cut off at the height of a railing: the building is closed and can still be looked into. The gate has posts of its own.
- **Every machine has a room.** The hall holds only what belongs to the machine on the turntable: the deck, the arms, the display and the pad at the gate. The Mechanic, the Archivist, the Smelter and the Tuner each work in a room behind a bay door, and each room is furnished for that work (section 7). Three of the doors are open from the start. The Tuner's has to be bought open.
- **The camera looks into a room over its long side.** That side is only a kerb. Rooms to the left and right of the hall are seen with the hall's own camera. The rooms behind the far wall would lie behind their own door wall from there, so while the robot is in one of them the camera turns about 40 degrees to look in from the side, and turns back in the hall. It takes under half a second.
- There are no enemies, and the room is small enough to cross in a few seconds.

The robot walks where the stick points, seen from the camera. Only the machine steers relative to itself. When the camera turns, a stick that is being held keeps meaning what it meant when it was pushed, so the robot does not swerve while the picture turns; the new direction counts from the next push.

| **Turntable** | The display | Machines: which of the two machines stands on the turntable, a copy for the free place, delete | Always there | First |
| **Bay 1** | The Mechanic | Build: put parts into the slots of the machine on the turntable, choose its frame, reactor and drive. Later also the Legacy part. | Awake from the start | First |
| **Bay 2** | The Archivist | Its screen has two sides. Parts: look through every part there is, found or not. | **Bay 3** | The Smelter | Frames: buy new ones and raise the Marks of owned ones, for Beats. Later also Marks and blueprints for other parts, and crafting a Warden's remains into a unique part. | Awake from the start in the prototype; in the story, when the first Core comes home | First |

## 4. Story beats and what they change

| Story beat | What changes in the Roundhouse | What the player gains |
|---|---|---|
| The first run ends | The Archivist's lamp comes on | The Codex |
| The first Core comes home | The Smelter fires up | Marks and blueprints |
| The first fusion is made | The Archivist uncovers the fusion pages, with every fusion as a silhouette | A list of what is left to find |
| Kiln falls | Kiln's husk is hauled inside. The Tuner wakes and the Echo Chamber door opens. | Workshop perks, Heat Echoes in the pool, and the Kiln Reactor as a craft |
| Each further Warden falls | Its husk takes a place along the wall | That strand's Echoes and one unique craft |
| All five strands are home | Bay 6 opens | The ending |

## 5. Mysteries

These hooks cost almost nothing to build and give the player something to wonder about from the first minute.

- **The sixth bay.** There are five strands and six bays. Bay 6 is sealed from the start and nobody will say why.
- **Blank pages.** Reactions and fusions the player has not found show in the Codex as blank plates. The Archivist gives one hint per visit, for example "Two cold things and a pull."
- **The hum.** Something under the turntable hums. It grows louder with every Warden that falls.
- **Changing remarks.** Each woken machine has a two-line remark that changes after every story beat. Read together, they show that the machines disagree about making the Pulse whole.

## 6. Rules for the machines in the lobby

- They are machines, not people. They do not walk.
- Each one has a lamp, and the lamp shows its status: off means dormant, steady means nothing new, pulsing means something new is waiting.
- A remark is at most two short lines.
- One machine has one job. If a new function is needed, a new machine wakes; an existing one does not get a second menu. The Smelter is the one exception, because upgrading and crafting use the same furnace.
- One machine has one room. The room shows what the machine does before its screen is opened: something in it is always at work.

## 7. Start small

The first version has the Gate and Bays 1 to 3. The machines are static models with a lamp. The panels are plain interface screens in the shared GUI style. Bays 4 to 6 exist as dark, closed shapes, so the player can see there is more to come.

Status: built in the Unity project. Four machines are awake, each in a room of its own behind a bay door: the Mechanic, the Archivist and the Smelter, whose doors are open from the start, and the Tuner, whose door the player buys open. The other two bay doors are on the camera's side, in the low part of the wall, and stay shut. Only usable things carry a hovering sign: the machines, the display at the turntable, the Tuner's door while it is locked, and the gate. The Beats the player has brought home are counted top right. Esc or Start opens the pause menu, which holds the options and the list of controls; the screens themselves carry no key hints. Nothing has to be saved by hand: a small green mark lights in the corner for a moment whenever the game has written the profile.

**The display** stands at the turntable, next to the machine. Its screen shows the player's machines side by side, each with its wiring diagram, its frame and Mark, its Power and what sits in its slots; the one on the turntable carries a green ACTIVE tag. "Take" puts the highlighted machine on the turntable. The free place takes a copy of the machine on the turntable. "Delete" removes a machine, but never the last one. Nothing is built here.

**The Mechanic** (called the Fitter until the seventh round) is where the machine on the turntable is built. Its screen has three columns, from the general to the particular. Left: the machine: its name, its wiring diagram and one row for its frame, each of its slots, its drive and its reactor. Middle: what fits the highlighted row. For a slot these are the kinds of part the player has, one line for each kind however many of it there are, with their number on the line. Right: the player's parts of the highlighted kind, one by one, each with its level, its rarity as notches and what its ability does; five Flamethrowers are five rows under the one line for the Flamethrower. With a single part of a kind there is nothing to choose, and the right column says what that part is. A part sits in one slot, and picking one that is already on the machine moves it. Choosing a part puts it in at once, and the diagram, the live wires and the Power count change with it, but only on the screen. "Save" makes it permanent. A machine that draws more Power than its reactor gives cannot be saved: the count turns red and the button is hatched. Putting the machine on another frame keeps every module that has a slot of the same name there and takes the rest off.

**The Mechanic's shop** lies behind the far wall, to the left of the gate. In it: a four-post lift with a bare frame on it, the next one the player could build, which is being welded in bursts; a crane with a reactor block turning on its chain; the weapons the player owns on three stands, turning slowly; brass gears that mesh and turn on the back wall next to a board of tools; a bench, a red roll cabinet, a compressor that shakes on its feet, a welding set, a stack of wheels, bins and barrels.

**The Archive** lies behind the far wall, to the right of the gate. In it: three walls of small drawers with a ladder that rolls slowly along them; a cabinet of cards with one drawer pulled out; a machine with two reels of tape that turn, stop and turn; glass cases along the kerb, each with a part the player has found turning in it; a round table with a lens that shows the found parts one after another as pictures of green light; a sloped reading desk with a lamp, a bin of rolled plans, and the old shelf with its reading place.

**The Foundry** lies to the right of the hall. A track runs across it, and behind the track is the hot end, where nobody walks: the furnace, half as large again as it used to be, with bellows that breathe and a stack that smokes, a bin of coal and the tools. A cart carries a crucible along the track from the mouth of the furnace to a bed of sand in which the shape of a frame is cut, pours, and rolls back; the bed glows up and throws sparks while it does. On the near side stand that bed, the rack where finished frames hang, a hammer that falls every second and a half on a glowing billet, a trough that steams, and ingots, the top ones still hot.

**The Archivist** shows every part the game has, by kind, each kind with a symbol of its own (a crosshair for weapons, a wired node for systems, a shield for armor, a wheel for drives, a cell for reactors, a chassis for frames): weapons, systems, support modules, drives, reactors and frames. A found part has a tile with its slot shape and state, and a data sheet with its tags, its description, its numbers as bars against the best part of its kind, and where it goes. A part that has not been found is a hatched tile, and its sheet only says which dungeon keeps it. A part found since the last visit carries a NEW mark until it has been looked at, and the Archivist's sign says how many are waiting.

**The Smelter** is where frames come from. Its furnace burns, and next to it stands a rack with a bare frame hanging from it. Its screen shows every frame on the left, hatched while the player does not own it; the slots of the highlighted frame in the middle; and its Marks on the right, each with the hull it gives and what it costs. One button does the next thing there is to do for that frame: buy it, or raise it by one Mark. With too few Beats it is hatched and says how many are missing. What a frame is, and what the three frames are, is in machine-concept.md, section 6.

**The Tuning Room** lies behind the fourth bay door, on the west side of the hall. Until it is bought, the door is a shutter with a lock of two arms across it, its lamps are grey, and nothing behind it exists. A sign over the door says what it costs, and standing in front of it brings up the prompt with the price; with too few Beats the key is grey and the price is red. Buying it plays a scene. The door stands side-on to the hall's camera, which would see nothing of it opening, so for the length of the scene the camera is the scene's own: it swings round in front of the door while the robot turns to watch, the lamps turn green, the two arms swing up against the posts, the shutter rattles and rolls up into the lintel with dust coming off it, and the room comes out of the dark as its lamps come on. Then the camera rises over the room, back into its usual angle, and returns to the robot. From then on the room is open for good, and the robot walks in through the doorway.

Any later room that lies behind a door follows the same three rules. The camera of its opening scene stands in front of the door. Nothing of the room is seen before its light is up, from wherever the camera looks: until then a cover in the colour of the dark around the building lies over it and thins out with the light. And the doorway is an area of its own that reaches well into the hall and well into the room, because the robot has to fit into one area with its whole width at every step (`LobbyWalk`).

The room is smaller than the hall and full: a floor of plates with grates and cable channels and warning marks at the door; the press in the middle of its back half, with nothing hanging over it, because the camera looks down into the room; two racks of parts and a board of tools along the back wall; a desk with three screens; a bench with a vise; bottles of gas; bins, crates and a reel of cable along the low side, which is only a kerb so that the camera can look in. The press comes down every few seconds onto one of the player's own parts, which turns in the light and throws sparks while it is worked. Steam comes out of a vent without a break, the screens flicker, and the Tuner's two arms never stop.

**The Tuner** shows the player's parts, grouped. On the left are the kinds of part the player owns, under a heading for weapons, for systems and for armor: one line for a kind, with how many of it there are. In the middle are the parts of the highlighted kind one by one, each named after what sets it apart (its ability, or "Plain") with its level and its rarity as notches. On the right is the highlighted part with its numbers now and one level up. One button raises it by a level for Beats; another scraps it for a few. A part that sits on a machine cannot be scrapped. Levels, abilities and rarities are in machine-concept.md, section 6.1.

## 8. The two sides of the Archivist's screen

The screen opens on its parts. One button in the bottom right corner turns it over to the Codex and back, and so does Q or the X button. The button says when something is waiting on the other side: "CODEX +85" means the Archivist owes 85 Beats. With a debt and no new part, the screen opens on the Codex by itself.

The Codex is laid out like the parts: three sections on the left (Reactions, Strays, Echoes) with how many of each are known, the entries of the highlighted section as tiles, and the sheet of the highlighted entry on the right. A reaction is drawn as its two strands side by side. A Stray is a head with eyes in the colour of the biome it lives in. An Echo is its sign. An entry the Archivist still owes for has the sum in a green tag in its corner; moving onto the tile pays it, and the Beats counter in the top right corner goes up. The sheet counts: how often a reaction went off, how many of a species were destroyed, and its mini boss and its boss by name once they have fallen.
